What We Automate

The Jobs Nobody Wants to Do Twice

These are the tasks that don't make you money but can't be skipped. They're also the ones a machine handles better than a person — because a machine doesn't get tired at page 300.

Submittal Registers

Specialty trade contractors on commercial and institutional work
Right now

A project engineer reads 400 pages of specifications and types every required submittal into a spreadsheet. One to two days per project. Miss one and it turns into a back-charge or a schedule delay.

After

The spec goes in, the register comes out — every submittal with its section reference, type, and stated deadline, and the long-lead items flagged. Minutes instead of days, exported straight into Procore or your existing log.

Certified Payroll

Anyone working Davis-Bacon or prevailing wage projects
Right now

Every week, on every job, somebody rebuilds WH-347 forms by hand from timecards. It has to reconcile exactly to payroll. Errors hold up payment and invite a closer look you don't want.

After

Timecards in, WH-347 out — reconciled, formatted for the awarding agency, ready to file. Every project, every week, without somebody's Friday disappearing into it.

Change Orders & Closeout

Document tracking from request through final payment
Right now

Documents live in email threads. Nobody's certain what's approved, what's still outstanding, or which missing piece of paper is holding up final payment on a job you finished two months ago.

After

Every document tracked from request to approval with a status anyone on the team can check. Closeout packages assemble themselves from what's already been collected.

Not a contractor?

The same thing applies anywhere documents move by hand. We build closing package assembly for title and settlement companies, and dues invoicing, renewals, and member record workflows for trade associations. If your team retypes information that already exists somewhere else, there's usually something here.
